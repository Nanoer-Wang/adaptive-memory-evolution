\begin{abstract}
Persistent memory must preserve useful history while responding to revised facts and changing demand. \emph{Adaptive Memory Evolution} (\ame) pairs content with evolving importance, consistency, usage, and temporal states. Frozen controller weights and rate interventions change behavior without retraining. Experiments show benefits under small capacity and interference, but not uniform superiority. Across four environments and 84 rate pairs, 27 exhibit opposing retention and adaptation changes significant after multiplicity correction. Replacement controls support attribution beyond counts, not full causal identification. Semantic transfer exposes stale retrieval despite preserved evidence. These findings characterize conditional stability--plasticity control.
\end{abstract}
