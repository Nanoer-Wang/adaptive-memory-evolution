\section{Conclusion}
Adaptive Memory Evolution makes the retention state of individual content records an explicit dynamical object. With frozen controller weights, rate interventions alter stable retention and post-shift adaptation, providing statistically supported operating-point choices in constrained non-stationary streams. Component and long-horizon analyses connect these choices to usage inertia and observed consistency, while dormant-recall and semantic-transfer failures expose their limits. The evidence supports a tunable memory-management mechanism; it does not establish LNN superiority, continuous integration necessity, universal improvement, or a general solution to lifelong agents.
